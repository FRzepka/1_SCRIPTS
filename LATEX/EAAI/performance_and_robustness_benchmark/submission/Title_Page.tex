\section*{Abstract}
Embedded battery-state estimators require validation under disturbed inputs and the inference semantics used on resource-constrained hardware. This study presents a causal, cell-disjoint benchmark of four State-of-Charge (SOC) approaches: fixed-capacity Coulomb counting as a direct-measurement model (DM), Coulomb counting with data-driven State-of-Health adaptation as a hybrid direct-measurement model (HDM), a second-order resistor-capacitor observer as a hybrid equivalent-circuit model (HECM), and a gated recurrent unit structurally pruned from $96$ to $67$ hidden units as the data-driven model (DD). Public lithium iron phosphate ageing data cover multiple operating and health conditions. Robustness evaluation addresses nominal accuracy, corrupted or unavailable measurements, and recovery from initialization errors. Hardware evaluation on an STM32H753 measures numerical equivalence, latency, flash, and peak runtime memory. For DD, exact rolling-window recomputation, continuous state forwarding, and periodic state reset are compared. Nominal cell-macro mean absolute errors are $0.0690$, $0.0412$, $0.0337$, and $0.0258$ for DM, HDM, HECM, and DD. DD yields the lowest nominal error and leads several disturbance scenarios, whereas HECM shows the strongest recovery. All implementations reproduce their software references, fit available memory, and meet the one-second sampling deadline. Compared with rolling-window recomputation, continuous-state DD reduces median latency from approximately $724$~ms to $0.425$~ms and peak memory from $67.3$~KiB to $4.1$~KiB while retaining similar nominal error. Periodic resets retain these resource benefits but cause pronounced transients. The results demonstrate that estimator choice, model compression, and recurrent-state handling must be evaluated jointly with accuracy, robustness, recovery, and embedded cost.

\textbf{Keywords:} State-of-charge estimation; Battery management system; Recurrent neural network; Robustness benchmarking; Embedded artificial intelligence; Model compression
