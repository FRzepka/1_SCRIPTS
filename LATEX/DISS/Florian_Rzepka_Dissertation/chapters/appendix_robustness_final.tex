\subsection{HECM lookup-table sensitivity}
\label{sec:appendix_hecm_lookup_sensitivity}

The cross-model benchmark evaluates one fixed HECM implementation whose lookup surfaces are identified exclusively from training and validation data. The supplementary HECM-only analysis tests whether its disturbance response depends strongly on local lookup calibration. The $R_i$, $R_1$, and $R_2$ surfaces are jointly scaled by $-10\%$ or $+10\%$. The $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ surfaces are likewise scaled by $-10\%$ or $+10\%$, while the OCV surfaces are shifted by $-\SI{10}{\milli\volt}$ or $+\SI{10}{\milli\volt}$. These six one-at-a-time perturbations and the nominal lookup are evaluated across 22 measurement and signal-integrity subcases and the paired initialization intervention. All original seeds and 16 frozen windows are retained. The complete design comprises 9184 HECM executions.

Main-campaign disturbances use their lookup-matched baseline, the one-hour dropout uses a lookup-matched 48-h baseline, and initialization recovery uses a separate paired baseline. For every disturbance, the lookup interaction is the scenario $\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ under the perturbed lookup minus the corresponding $\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ under the nominal lookup. Results are summarized within cells before the same equal-weight cell bootstrap used by the primary benchmark is applied.

Local lookup changes shift baseline HECM MAE between $0.0314$ and $0.0373$, compared with $0.0337$ for the nominal lookup. Across the 22 disturbance subcases, the largest absolute interaction is $0.006116$ MAE. It occurs for the $-\SI{0.05}{A}$ current offset under $+10\%$ time-constant scaling, and its cell-bootstrap interval includes zero. This interaction is small relative to the nominal adverse current-offset penalty of $0.1763$ MAE. The largest current-gain interaction is $0.000443$ MAE.

Initialization recovery exposes a separate boundary. Five perturbations retain mean persistent recovery-or-censor times between $1.18$ and $1.41$~h, close to the nominal $1.20$~h. Scaling the resistance surfaces by $-10\%$ increases the equal-weight mean to $5.10$~h because one low-load holdout trajectory remains outside the recovery band for the 24-h horizon. The analysis supports local stability of most measurement-disturbance responses but does not establish lookup independence of baseline accuracy or observer re-anchoring.
